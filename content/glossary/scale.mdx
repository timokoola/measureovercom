  - q: "What is the difference between scale and proportion?"
    a: "Scale is the relationship between the drawing and the world — half size, twice size, 1:100. Proportion is the relationship of parts within the drawing to each other. You can draw at the wrong scale and still be perfectly proportioned; the reverse is what looks wrong."
  - q: "Do I need a reference object to work at a known scale?"
    a: "To tie a drawing to real-world dimensions, yes — something in the photo whose actual size you know. ## In practice

Scale becomes a problem the moment a drawing has to meet something physical: a
frame, a stencil that must fit a surface, a pattern piece cut to size. For work
that stays on the page, proportion carries almost all the weight and scale is
just a decision about how big to go.

## Consistency is the whole game

A drawing at a slightly wrong scale, applied evenly, still reads as correct —
the viewer has nothing to compare against. A drawing whose scale drifts across
the page does not, and the eye catches it immediately even when it cannot say
why. Measuring against a fixed sheet size is how you stop the drift.
