## Why measure at all

The eye is good at judging angles and poor at judging distance across a picture
plane. Most drawings that "look off" are not badly rendered — they are
correctly rendered in the wrong place. Fixing position before rendering is far
cheaper than fixing it after.

## A worked order

Fix the outer bounds, then the largest interior division, then anything that
must be right for the drawing to read: the eyeline of a portrait, the spring of
an arch, the waist of a figure. Everything else can be judged by eye once those
are anchored.

## When not to bother

Loose gestural work does not want this, and neither does anything you intend to
abstract. Reach for it when the drawing has to match something — a commission, a
stencil that must fit, a pattern piece cut to size.
