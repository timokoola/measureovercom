## Reduce before you measure

A stencil is a two-tone object. Work out which areas are cut and which are left
before placing a single guide line — grayscale and contrast help here, because
they make it obvious where a mid-tone is going to have to commit one way or the
other.

## Position the shapes, not the outlines

The instinct is to trace edges. For a stencil what matters is where each shape
sits relative to its neighbours: a mouth two millimetres low reads as a
different face. Put your guide lines through the anchors — eye corners, the
centre line, the outer bounds — and let the outlines follow.

## Bridges are structure

Anywhere an island would float free needs a bridge, and bridges are easier to
plan on screen than to rescue mid-cut. Mark them as guide lines so their
positions transfer with everything else.
